The last two weeks of October change what a costume has to do. It stops being a party outfit and becomes something worn on a dark street. Buyers start asking about glow, reflectivity and pale colourways — features that are cheap to add at sampling and almost impossible to add later.

## What the threads and guides keep pointing at

- **Glow is asked for as a costume idea in its own right.** A thread on glowstick costume ideas is people building the light into the look rather than hiding it.
- **Light-up is usually added at home.** One parent hot-glued a strand of LEDs to a child's clothes with the battery in a pocket; makers in another thread describe electroluminescent wire as the same idea, more expensively.
- **Reflective tape on the costume and the bag** is the remedy repeated across public and school safety guidance.
- **Lighter colourways are recommended over dark ones**, which is a colour-range decision rather than a styling one.
- **The ask arrives from the parent, not the wearer**, and usually in the last fortnight.

## Why visibility has to be a sampling decision

- **Reflective tape is applied, not added later** — if it is missing, the buyer buys tape and sticks it on, and the costume reads homemade.
- **Glow-in-the-dark is either a print or a pigment in the yarn**, so it has to be chosen with the fabric, not after it.
- **Pale and dark colourways cannot share one spec**, and the darkest suits are the hardest case because they read dark by design.
- **The unit cost is small; the after-market fix is not**, and nothing on an unlit costume looks broken in inspection.

## The visibility specs to write into the order

- **Specify reflectivity where the garment faces traffic** — hem, sleeve, shoulder, and the bag that swings beside the body.
- **State whether the glow is a print or an applied patch**, because the two age differently across washes.
- **Test the glow after washing**, not only on the sealed sample.
- **Keep pale colourways in the range**, and price them rather than dropping them.
- **Check the wearer's own vision too** — eye openings, hood depth, and a hem that clears the stairs.
- **Photograph the sample in low light**, not only on white, so the buyer sees what they are approving.

## Why it pays

- **It is the feature late buyers ask for by name**, and one of the few they will pay a little more for.
- **A treat bag or reflective accessory is a natural attach-on**, raising units per order without a new pattern.
- **It answers the plainness complaint**, which is what a fully dark costume triggers on a parent's first look.
- **It survives re-wear**, so the costume still gets used after the 31st.
